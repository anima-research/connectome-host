- **Readline mode (`--no-tui` with stdin and stdout on a terminal):** a paste arrives as one message, as the person made it but for whitespace at its start and end. The host turns the terminal's bracketed paste on at its prompt, and off again when the prompt closes (`/quit`, Ctrl-D, Ctrl-C), when the process exits, and at a SIGTERM, before the signal ends it. The lines of a paste are held, and the person's next Enter sends them, with what that Enter ends, as one message; after a paste that held lines, a dim line says how many and that Enter sends them. A message of one line acts as typed, so one starting with `/` runs as a command however it got onto the prompt, and a message of more than one line, which only a paste makes, goes to the agent as text whatever its lines start with. A terminal that doesn't support bracketed paste sends its lines one by one, as before. A line typed while the agent's turn runs waits for the turn after it, and lines entered together, as a terminal without bracketed paste sends a paste, are answered in order, each in a turn of its own, a command among them running as it's read; a line still waiting to go in when the person asks for a session switch isn't sent, and the host says so, naming its start, and when the conversation ends the host says how many lines hadn't gone in; and the prompt is drawn once nothing the person sent is still waiting for its answer, so each answer prints before the prompt that follows it; before, the prompt could come before an answer and none after it. A line's 120 s limit counts from when nothing sent before it, setup's opening included, is still waited on, and not while setup asks for a secret, and an answer that comes after its line's limit, or one to no line, such as a wake's, is followed by the prompt again. A line whose handling fails is said to have failed, with why, and the host goes on with the prompt; before, it ended readline mode.
